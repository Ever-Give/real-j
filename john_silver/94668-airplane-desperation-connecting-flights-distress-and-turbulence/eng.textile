This is a time from some time ago when I was flying a two-leg flight, both flights being about 3 h long.

Note: I tagged this as male + female. I'll tell what happened to me, but there were females that were in the same situaltion, too. I won't describe them further, however.

Flying has a strange effect on my bladder. No matter what I do, I always end up being needing to pee when I board the plane. Maybe it is a psychological thing, maybe air pressure has something to do with it, I don't know. I have tried to minimize fluid intake, but this results in concentrated urine, that causes some distress, too. And finally, being in a cramped space, without the possibility to always use the toilet, makes me quite anxious - since I had a habit of wetting as a kid, I have developed a kind of awareness of the toilets, and whenever I don't know where they are or am unable to use them, I am at unease.

Anyway, before boarding the first flight, I made sure to use the toilet at the airport. I have concluded that some coffee and juice onboard is the best middle road for me; drink some but not too much, and I did just that. I also consumed a large coffee at the airport, which was something that I don't usually do, and should have skipped this time, too. The first flight was delayed, so what would happen to the connecting flight was unknown at the point, and at the desk they didn't know anything about it.

After boarding, I didn't really need to go before they started shuffling with the trays, serving drinks etc (well, not more than that usual need that I always have), and at that time it is always hard to go to the toilet anyway. So I ordered my coffee and juice and figured to go after the hassle then. Unfortunately for me, that's where the turbulence started, so the rest of the flight was basically under "fasten your seat belt"-sign. There was a brief period of non-turbulent flight, but I missed that oportunity, too, don't really remember the reason, might have been that my need was not so bad yet. However, the end result was that the rest of the flight was "toilets closed", that resulted in quite a discomfort. Not in a way of close-to-accident-type, but rather a feeling that something is about to leak any second, pain in the lower stomach-type, which was very uncomfortable and that I didn't like at all.

The connecting flight was unfortunately on time, and that ment that quite a lot of people had to rush very quickly to the other terminal, and there definately was no time to use toilets on the airport. On the second plane, there was the typical delays etc., and it took over half an hour before the plane started taxiing, all of the time toilets unavailable.

Turbulance is caused by weather, and logic dictates that the weather after was the same than weather before. Turbulance again. Ouch. Nearly an hour of it. Ouch again. I kept squirming on my seat, mind you, not nearly accident-level, but at a very significant discomfort nonetheless, staring at the seat belt sign. My seat was on the frontish side, and I had decided to use the loo immediately when the sign went of, no fooling around any more. I did not count the time, but it must have been around 5 hours since I had last used the toilet, and I was not comfortable with that, to say the least.

Eventually the light went off, and I immediately begun to proceed to the front toilet, only to be stopped by a flight attendant. "Toilet", I explained, but she told that unfortunately the only available toilets were at the back (front one was for the business class passengers, I later found out). So, I walked to the back of the airplane, that had an immense toilet line of around 10 people, and I took the last place, wondering when the turbulence would hit again.

That same flight attendant passed by the toilet line and cheerfully told me "Quite a line we have to the toilets today." I paused, looked at her eyes for a second or two, thouhgt for a sec, and then, with my sweetest and most polite voice said: "It figures. We had to rush from the other flight, and that already had lots of turbulence." She replied: "Oh." and I could almost hear when pieces of puzzle clicked in place in her mind. "I'll see what I can do," she continued and went on her way. Shortly after she returned, and said she would make an exception and let two of us use the front lavatory, after all. With a sweet and polite voice, that would have left Engilsh Lords at shame, I replied "Thank you so much", and proceeded to the front toilet, although first making sure that no kids were in the line (weren't) nor other adults in clear distress (weren't either). I was second to the front lavatory and had a relief of a lifetime.

And what do you know, just when I stepped out, the turbulence struck again, and lasted for the rest of the flight.
